## Repository Structure

Each experiment has its own folder with the same layout:

- 'experiment/' jsPsych code for the online experiment 
- 'data/' anonymized data 
- 'analysis' R analysis script 
- 'figures' plots

## Experiments
### Experiment 1: Equal_cost_gender
https://web.mit.edu/anadiz/www/divisions-of-labor/

Participants read about two characters who share two recurring tasks that are described as equal in effort and value. The characters can coordinate completing the tasks by alternating (both take turns doing both tasks), dividing the labor (each person specializes in only one tasks), or one person does both task. Participants rated how hierarchical they consider the relationship before and after receiving information about how the characters coordinated the tasks.

### Experiment 2: Certainty
https://web.mit.edu/anadiz/www/certainty/

Participants read about two characters who share two recurring tasks that are described as equal in effort and value. Participants also read the coordination pattern either alternating (both take turns doing both tasks), or dividing the labor (each person specializes in only one tasks), replicating posterior ratings from Experiment 1 (prior). Then, participants received information about whether one character “continues to agree that the tasks are equal in effort and value” (agree) or “has started to wonder whether the tasks are really as equal in effort and value” (uncertainty), and rated the relationship again (posterior).
Unlike, Experiment 1, there is not "one person does all" condition.

### Experiment 3: 
https://web.mit.edu/anadiz/www/efficiency2/

Like the past two experiments participants read about two characters who share two recurring tasks that are described as equal in effort and value. Participants  viewed the coordination pattern and a statement indicating either that dividing the tasks "saves a great deal of time and effort" compared to alternating (high efficiency gain) or "only a little time and effort" compared to alternating (low efficiency gain). Participants then provided a single hierarchy rating for each scenario. For one scenario per condition, we also included a written "explain your answer" box to gain insight into the reasoning participants used and which parts of the vignette contributed to their judgments.

## Issues 
In the certainty and the efficiency experiments, "condition assignment", was fixed at 0, so all participants saw the same scenario-to-condition. Scenario is therefore cofunded with condition. 

I did not make any new arrangements on the data analysis after finding out about the counterbalancing mistake.
