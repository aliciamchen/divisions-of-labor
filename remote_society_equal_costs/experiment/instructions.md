Page 1
In this study we are interested in how we can make inferences about other people's relationships by observing their behavior. We are specifically interested in how people evaluate how hierarchical a relationship is: how different two people are in rank, importance, or influence. 

Page 2
Imagine that you are traveling very far away, and you meet a new group of people you know nothing about. You are trying to learn about the people, the society, and the language, all at the same time. 

In this study, you will read 6 scenarios. Each scenario describes two people who regularly interact around two recurring activities or exchanges. You will observe how they divide these between them.

Page 3
First, based only on the scenario description, we will ask you to estimate how hierarchical the relationship might be. Then, we will show you how the two people actually divide the activities or exchanges, and ask you again.

We are interested in how people use just brief observation to make judgments about the type of relationship from observed social interactions.
